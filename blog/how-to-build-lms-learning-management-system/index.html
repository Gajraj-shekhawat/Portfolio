How to Build an LMS: Features Checklist for Courses and Tests

Whether you sell courses or train your staff, here's what a learning management system needs, what to build first, and when to use an existing one instead.

A learning management system (LMS) lets people take courses, watch lessons, attempt tests and track progress. I worked on the backend of an online learning platform where thousands of students attend live classes and tests at the same time. Here's the checklist I'd use to plan one.

Ready-made or custom?

  • Ready-made (Moodle, Teachable, Thinkific and similar): fastest for standard online courses.
  • Custom: when you need your own branding and app, exam-style tests, special pricing or batches, or deep integration with admissions and fees.

Core features

  • Courses with modules and lessons: video, PDFs, notes.
  • Secure video streaming with adaptive quality for slow connections.
  • Live classes with recordings available afterwards.
  • Tests and quizzes: timers, question types, negative marking, instant results, rank lists.
  • Progress tracking and certificates on completion.
  • Payments: one-time, subscriptions or batch fees, with coupons.
  • Roles: students, teachers, admins, and parents if needed.
  • Mobile app or a mobile-first website.

Planning for scale

  • Live classes and tests create sudden peaks: thousands of students at the same minute. Design the backend and database for that peak, not the average.
  • Serve video through a CDN, never from your own server.
  • Use caching and queues for results and notifications (background jobs).

Launch checklist

  • Load-test a full test with the number of students you expect.
  • Prevent account sharing (device limits, session checks) if you sell paid content.
  • Back up data daily and monitor errors during live events.
