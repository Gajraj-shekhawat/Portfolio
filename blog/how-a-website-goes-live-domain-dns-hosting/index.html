How a Website Goes Live: Domain, DNS and Hosting Explained

Domain, DNS, hosting, SSL: four words every website owner hears. Here's what each one is, using an everyday comparison, and what you should own yourself.

If you're planning a website and the jargon feels confusing, this guide is for you. No coding needed. Think of your website like a shop.

The four pieces

  • Domain (yourbusiness.com) is your shop's address. You rent it yearly from a registrar such as Cloudflare, Namecheap or GoDaddy.
  • Hosting is the building where your website's files live: a server or a service that shows your pages to visitors.
  • DNS is the signboard and directions: settings that tell the internet "this address points to that building", and where your email goes.
  • SSL (the padlock, https) is the lock on the door: it encrypts traffic between visitors and your site. Today it's usually free.

How a visit works

  1. Someone types yourbusiness.com.
  2. Their device asks DNS: "where does this domain live?"
  3. DNS replies with your host's address.
  4. The host sends back your web page over a secure connection.

All of this takes a fraction of a second.

What you should own yourself

  • The domain, registered in your name and account. This is the most important one.
  • The hosting account, or at least admin access to it.
  • The code in a repository you own (for example GitHub).
  • Your business email account.

Developers can be added as team members to all of these. If someone else owns your domain, moving on later becomes difficult.

Typical yearly costs

  • A .com domain usually costs about the same as a meal out per year; watch for cheap first-year offers with expensive renewals.
  • Hosting for a simple, fast business site can be free or very low cost on modern static hosting.
  • SSL is free with most hosts today.
  • Business email ranges from free forwarding to a paid mailbox per user.
