DNS Setup for Beginners: A, CNAME, MX and TXT Records

DNS settings look scary but there are only a few record types you'll ever need. Here's what each one does and how to set them safely.

DNS records are the instructions that connect your domain to your website and email. You edit them in your domain registrar or DNS provider's dashboard (for example Cloudflare). Here are the only records most businesses ever need.

The common record types

  • A record: points a name (like yourbusiness.com) to a server's IPv4 address, such as 203.0.113.10. Use it when your host gives you an IP address.
  • AAAA record: the same, for an IPv6 address.
  • CNAME record: points a name to another name, such as www → yourbusiness.pages.dev. Use it when your host gives you a hostname.
  • MX records: say which servers receive your email. Your email provider tells you exactly what to add.
  • TXT records: text used to prove you own the domain (Google Search Console, email providers) and to protect email from spoofing (SPF, DKIM, DMARC).

Connecting a website, step by step

  1. In your hosting service, add your domain. It will show which records to create.
  2. In your DNS dashboard, add exactly those records (usually an A record for the main domain and a CNAME for www).
  3. Wait a few minutes; sometimes changes take up to a few hours to spread everywhere.
  4. Open your site with and without www and check the padlock (https) appears.

Connecting email

  1. Add the MX records your email provider gives you, and remove old ones.
  2. Add the SPF TXT record and turn on DKIM in your email provider.
  3. Add a basic DMARC TXT record so others can't easily send email pretending to be you.
  4. Send a test email to a Gmail address and check it doesn't land in spam.

Safety tips

  • Take a screenshot of your existing records before changing anything.
  • Change one thing at a time and test it.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication on your registrar and DNS accounts; whoever controls DNS controls your website and email.
