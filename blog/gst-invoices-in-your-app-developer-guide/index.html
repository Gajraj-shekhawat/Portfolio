GST Invoices in Your App: What Developers Must Get Right

Invoices are legal documents. These are the details that matter when your app generates them automatically. Always confirm rules with your accountant.

When I built ordering apps for a wholesaler, every order needed a proper GST invoice generated automatically. Getting the details right saves your accountant hours every month and avoids awkward corrections. This is a developer's checklist, not tax advice: confirm the rules for your business with a chartered accountant.

What goes on every invoice

  • Seller's name, address and GSTIN; buyer's details and GSTIN for B2B sales.
  • A unique, sequential invoice number, usually restarting each financial year with a prefix (for example NKS/2026-27/000123).
  • Invoice date and place of supply (the state).
  • For each line: description, HSN or SAC code, quantity, unit, rate, taxable value and tax rate.
  • Tax split and totals, amount in words, and a signature or authorisation note.

CGST + SGST or IGST?

If the seller and the place of supply are in the same state, tax is split equally into CGST and SGST. If they are in different states, it is charged as IGST. Your code should decide this from the seller's state and the place of supply, never from a manual choice.

Engineering rules

  1. Store money as whole paise (integers), not floating-point rupees, and apply one documented rounding rule.
  2. Generate invoice numbers inside a database transaction so two orders can never get the same number and there are no gaps.
  3. Never edit an issued invoice. Corrections and returns are made with credit notes linked to the original.
  4. Save a snapshot of prices, tax rates and addresses on the invoice, so later changes to products don't change old invoices.
  5. Generate the PDF on the server and keep a copy; send it to the customer by email or WhatsApp.
  6. Check e-invoicing and e-way bill requirements for your turnover and goods with your accountant, and plan the integration if they apply.
