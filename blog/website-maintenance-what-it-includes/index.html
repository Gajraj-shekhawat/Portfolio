Website Maintenance: What It Includes and Why You Need It

A website isn't finished at launch. Here's what ongoing maintenance covers, in plain words, so you know what you're paying for.

Clients sometimes ask why they should pay for maintenance once the website is built. The honest answer: software doesn't sit still. Browsers change, libraries get security fixes, certificates renew, and your business changes too. Here's what good maintenance covers.

The essentials

  • Security updates for the framework, plugins and server.
  • Backups of the site and data, and a tested way to restore them (the 3-2-1 rule).
  • Uptime monitoring, so someone knows within minutes if the site goes down.
  • SSL and domain renewals, so the padlock never disappears and the domain never lapses.
  • Fixing what breaks after a browser or payment gateway update.

Useful extras

  • Small content changes: prices, photos, new pages.
  • A monthly check of speed and Google Search Console errors.
  • Updating forms, emails and WhatsApp links when your team changes.

How to budget

For most small business websites, a modest monthly plan or a block of hours per quarter works well. Apps with payments or many users need more, because there's more that can break and more at stake when it does. Whatever you choose, agree in writing what's included and how fast urgent issues get fixed.

What you should always keep

Admin access to your domain, hosting, code repository and email, so you're never stuck if a developer disappears (what to own yourself).
