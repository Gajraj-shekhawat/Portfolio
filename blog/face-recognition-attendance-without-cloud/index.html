Face-Recognition Attendance Without Sending Photos to the Cloud

One photo of the class, and every student is marked. No cloud AI, no photos leaving the school, and the teacher still has the final say.

Taking attendance eats several minutes of every period. For a school I worked with, I built a tool where the teacher takes one photo (or a short video) of the class and the system marks who is present.

How it works for the teacher

  1. Each student is enrolled once with 2 to 5 photos.
  2. In class, the teacher takes a photo or a short video.
  3. Every student comes back as Present, Needs your check or Not found.
  4. The teacher only reviews the last two groups. Usually that's a handful of students.

That middle state matters. I didn't want the system to guess when it wasn't sure. A teacher confirming three faces takes seconds; a wrong mark erodes trust in the whole tool.

The technology

Face detection uses OpenCV's YuNet model and recognition uses SFace, both running on an ordinary CPU. No paid cloud AI is involved, and no photo leaves the school's server.

Choosing a model by its licence

There are more accurate face models available. Some of the popular ones, though, come with weights licensed for non-commercial use only. For a tool used by a school that charges fees, that's a real risk. I picked models whose licences clearly allow this use, and tuned the workflow (multiple enrolment photos, the review step) to make up the accuracy difference.

Privacy by design

  • The recognition service stores no names and has no database. It receives images and face templates, and returns matches.
  • Student records stay in the school's own system.
  • It runs at low priority so it never slows down the other school apps.

If you're considering AI for a school or office, start with something small and measurable like this. See AI development or get in touch.
