How to Write a Software Brief: A Template for Founders

A one-page brief gets you better quotes, faster, from any developer. Copy this template and fill it in before you talk to anyone.

The best projects I've worked on started with a client who could explain their problem in a page. You don't need technical words for this; plain language is better. Copy the sections below and fill them in.

1. The problem

Two or three sentences on what's going wrong today and what it costs you. Example: "Retailers order by phone and WhatsApp. We write orders on paper and retype them into billing. We lose orders and make pricing mistakes every week."

2. Who will use it

List each type of user and what they need to do. Example: shop owners (place orders, see past orders), depot staff (see and pack orders), delivery drivers (see their route), owner (see sales).

3. Must-haves for version one

At most five things. If the product can't launch without it, it's a must-have. Everything else goes in the next list.

4. Nice-to-haves for later

Reports, extra languages, loyalty points, an iOS app. Writing them down stops them sneaking into version one.

5. Examples you like

Links to apps or websites whose look or flow you like, and one line on why.

6. What already exists

Your current website, domain, software, spreadsheets, and who has access to them.

7. Budget and deadline

A range is fine. Mention any fixed date, like an admission season or a festival sale.

8. How you'll measure success

One or two numbers: fewer phone orders, online fee collection, enquiries per week.

Send a brief like this to two or three developers and compare how they respond (what to ask them). Or send it to me; I'll reply with questions, a plan and a fixed quote.
