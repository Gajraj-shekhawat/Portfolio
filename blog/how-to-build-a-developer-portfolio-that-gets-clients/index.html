How to Build a Developer Portfolio That Gets Clients

Most developer portfolios list technologies. The ones that bring work show problems solved for real people. What I put in mine, and why.

When I rebuilt my own portfolio, I treated it like a client project: who visits, what do they need to see, and what should they do next? Here's what I learned.

1. Say who you help in one line

"Full-stack developer" describes thousands of people. "I build the software, apps and AI that real businesses run on" tells a business owner they're in the right place. Write for the person who pays, not for other developers.

2. Case studies, not screenshots

For each project, write the problem, what you built and the result, ideally with a number: orders moved from paper to apps, API latency cut by 30%, a test platform used by real students. Link to the live product if you can. You can see how I do it on the Neem Karoli Sales page.

3. Make contact effortless

  • A short contact form, plus email and WhatsApp.
  • A clear promise, such as "I usually reply within a day".
  • A downloadable resume for recruiters.

4. Make it fast

A slow portfolio from a developer is a bad sign. Mine scores 99–100 on PageSpeed because it's plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, with compressed images and self-hosted fonts (how).

5. Make Google understand who you are

  • Your full name in the title and main heading of the home page.
  • An About page about you, with the different ways people write your name.
  • Person structured data (JSON-LD) linking to your LinkedIn, GitHub and other profiles.
  • A sitemap, Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • Your website link on every profile you have.

6. Pages for what clients search

Separate pages for each service (e-commerce, backend, AI, websites) and a blog with useful articles bring visitors who are already looking for help. That's exactly why this blog exists.

7. Keep it current

Add each new project and update your resume. A portfolio last touched three years ago tells clients you might be gone too.
