Building a B2B Ordering App for a Wholesaler: 5 Lessons

What I learned moving a Jaipur beverage wholesaler from phone and paper orders to its own apps.

Neem Karoli Sales, a beverage wholesaler in Jaipur, took every order by phone and WhatsApp, wrote it on paper and re-typed it into billing. I built the whole platform: a shop app for retailers, a staff app for the depot and delivery team, a web admin and the API behind them. These are the lessons that mattered most.

1. Calculate every price on the server

Retail apps can get away with showing prices from the app. Wholesale can't: prices change often, schemes and discounts stack, and a one-rupee rounding error repeated across thousands of cases becomes a real dispute. Every total is calculated on the server in whole paise, never in the app, so the shop, the staff and the invoice always agree.

2. Orders are a state machine

An order moves through clear steps: placed, confirmed, packed, out for delivery, delivered, or cancelled. Writing these as an explicit state machine, with rules about who can move an order to which step, removed a whole class of bugs and made the staff app simple to use.

3. Sign-in that works for shop owners

Shop owners don't want passwords. Phone number plus OTP, run by our own backend, with SMS first and WhatsApp as a fallback, made sign-in reliable even where SMS delivery is patchy.

4. Payments and GST invoices

Payments go through Cashfree, and every payment is re-checked with the gateway on the server before an order is marked paid. Each order produces a GST invoice PDF with HSN codes and the CGST/SGST split, so the business's accounts stay clean without extra work.

5. Ship updates without waiting for an app store

The Android apps update themselves, with forced updates for important fixes and the ability to roll back a bad release. Combined with 1,290+ automated tests around pricing, payments and order flow, that means changes go out quickly without breaking orders.

Is this right for your business?

If your shops or distributors still order by phone, a simple ordering app pays for itself quickly. See e-commerce and B2B ordering development, or tell me how you take orders today.
