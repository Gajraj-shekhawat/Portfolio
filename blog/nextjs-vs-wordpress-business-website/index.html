Next.js vs WordPress for a Business Website in 2026

Both can build a great website. The right choice depends on who will edit it, how fast it must be, and what else it needs to do.

Clients often ask me whether their website should be built on WordPress or on a modern framework like Next.js. Both are good tools. Here is how I decide.

Choose WordPress when

  • Your team wants to edit pages and publish posts every week without a developer.
  • The site is mostly content: pages, a blog, a contact form.
  • You want a large choice of ready-made themes and plugins.

The catch: every plugin adds weight and something to keep updated. A WordPress site needs regular updates and good hosting to stay fast and secure.

Choose Next.js when

  • Speed matters: pages can be pre-built and served almost instantly, which helps Google rankings and conversions on mobile.
  • The website is part of a product: logins, dashboards, payments, online tests or ordering.
  • You want fewer moving parts to patch, and lower hosting costs for a mostly static site.
  • You may grow into an app later and want one codebase.

The catch: content editing needs a CMS (a headless CMS, or a simple admin panel) set up by your developer.

SEO: is one better?

Google doesn't rank a site higher because of the tool it was built with. It ranks pages that load fast, work well on phones and answer the searcher's question. Both can do this; Next.js makes speed easier to get, WordPress makes publishing easier to keep up.

My rule of thumb

  • Brochure site + frequent blogging by your team → WordPress, kept lean.
  • Fast landing pages, online payments, portals or anything app-like → Next.js.
  • Local shop or clinic that needs to be live this week → a fast static site with WhatsApp and Google Business Profile, which is what my business website in 7 days offer is.
